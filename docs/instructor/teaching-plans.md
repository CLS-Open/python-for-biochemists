# Teaching plans

Timings, routes and the checklist for running this course. The [instructor pack
overview](index.md) covers the misconceptions this course breaks, what to mark, and the
questions students actually ask.

## Before the session

1. **Install in advance, or bring a container.** `pip install -e ".[dev]"` needs `numpy` and
   `scipy` wheels; on a locked-down teaching cluster this is the step that eats the first hour.
   Test the install on the actual machines, not on yours.
2. **Run the validation once yourself.** `python scripts/validate_against_scipy.py` and
   `python -m pytest -q`. If the tests fail on the teaching machines, the session becomes a
   debugging session, which is a different (and worse) lesson.
3. **No network is required** for Modules 1–6, the practicals or the notebook. Only
   `check_references.py` needs the internet. Plan for offline — it is the common case.
4. **Print the practicals.** Students annotate the tables; the numbers they write down are the
   assessment evidence.

## Single day (6 hours teaching)

| Time | What | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Installation check, `kinetics --version` | Anyone still broken pairs with someone working |
| 0:15 | Module 1 + Practical 1 Steps 0–2 | Insist on units before any fitting |
| 1:15 | Module 2 (Vmax as extrapolation) | Ask what substrate concentration gives 0.99·Vmax; the answer (99·Km) lands |
| 2:15 | Module 3 + Practical 2 Steps 1–3 | The 200-seed simulation runs in about a second; run it live |
| 3:45 | Module 4 + Practical 3 | The pseudoreplication result is the day's high point |
| 5:00 | Module 6 (model choice, the non-claim) | Leave the `inhibition_weak` result on screen and say nothing |
| 5:45 | Wrap: what would you do with your own data? | Collect one sentence each |

Weighting (Module 5, Practical 4) is the natural casualty of a one-day format. It is also the
one with the largest numerical consequence, so if you must cut, cut Module 2's worked
derivation rather than the LOD exercise.

## Two-day format

Split the single day at the lunch break and add the material a day buys you:

| Day | Content | Why this boundary |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Modules 1–3, Practicals 1–2 | Ends with the student having reproduced the Lineweaver–Burk bias themselves |
| 2 | Modules 4–6, Practicals 3–5, quiz | Ends with the non-claim, which is the transferable skill |

The two-day format is strongly preferred. Students arrive on day 2 with a working environment, a
fit they have already broken, and a question about it.

## Half-day workshop (3 hours)

Module 3 §3.3–3.5, Practical 2 Steps 1–3, Module 6 §6.3 and §6.7. Skip the derivations; keep the
measures. Suitable for research groups who already fit curves and want to know what their error
bars mean.

## Course routes by cohort

| Cohort | Route | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Undergraduates, 3rd year biochemistry | Modules 1–4 + Practicals 1–3 | 8–10 h |
| MSc / graduate rotation | Whole course + Practical 5 + mini-project | 20–24 h |
| Statistics or data-science students | Modules 3–6 + Practicals 2, 3, 4 | 12 h |
| Research staff, one-day workshop | Module 3 + Practical 2 + Module 6 | 5 h |

## Running the quiz

The quiz is closed book with one page of notes and takes 60 minutes. Questions 1–6 test
interpretation without a terminal, which is the point: a student who can only answer by running
`kinetics` has not internalised anything.

Two answers are marked strictly and should be explained before the quiz:

* Question 13 (the `inhibition_weak` non-claim) scores 0 if a mechanism is named.
* Question 15 requires all three readings of the α output; two of three earns half marks.

## Adapting the material

* The datasets are generated by `scripts/make_datasets.py`, which is deterministic and seeded.
  Changing the seed is a legitimate exercise: *"does the conclusion survive a different
  dataset?"* is a better question than any worksheet.
* [`docs/limitations.md`](../limitations.md) is written to be handed out as-is. It is the honest
  answer to "what can't this do", and it is deliberately longer than the feature list.
* If you use this in a course, the only attribution asked for is the repository URL (CC BY 4.0
  for content, MIT for code).
